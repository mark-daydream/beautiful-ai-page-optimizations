H2: Pro Tips for Map Slides

Here are a couple of things to remember when putting together a map slide:

Tell a geographic story Use map slides to show movement, growth, or relationships between locations. A clear narrative—from global to local or region to region—helps audiences understand the significance behind the geography.

Prioritize key locations Highlight the most important offices, customers, markets, or opportunities with labeled markers. Focusing attention on a handful of meaningful points keeps the map clear and impactful.

Use zoom intentionally Zooming can create powerful visual context, but every transition should support the story. Guide viewers from the big picture to specific areas where insights, opportunities, or decisions are concentrated.

H2: World map template FAQ

Is the world map template editable?

Yes. The template is a fully editable Smart Slide. Change your locations, markers, and labels, and the layout adapts automatically as you work.

How do I add my own data to the world map template?

Open the template and type your locations, markers, and labels in, or upload a document or data file and Beautiful.ai places them on the slide.

Can AI fill in the world map template for me?

Yes. Describe the world map you want to show and the AI assistant fills the template with your locations, markers, and labels in place. You can keep editing everything afterward.

Can I export the world map template to PowerPoint or Google Slides?

Yes. Slides export to PowerPoint, Google Slides, or PDF, and you can also present or share a live link directly from Beautiful.ai.

When should I use a world map?

Use a world map when the where matters as much as the what, such as sales territories, customer locations, or a market expansion plan. Zooming from the globe into one region walks the audience through the geography instead of listing places.

The video ID is the folder in the player’s data-player-src (the playlist.m3u8 URL), contentUrl is Bunny’s MP4 rendition of that ID (play_720p.mp4 is the highest one the encode produced), the first thumbnailUrl is the poster image the player already shows, the second is Bunny’s own frame, duration is read from the HLS playlist and uploadDate is the date Bunny processed the video (swap in the library’s upload date if it differs).
