What is a sales presentation?

A sales presentation is the slide deck a sales team uses to move a deal or a plan forward. Some are client facing, like the proposal you walk a buyer through or the case study that shows what you delivered for a customer like them. Others never leave the building, like the strategy deck a manager presents to the team or the stand-up that keeps everyone tracking the same numbers.

Both kinds do the same job. They package your story for one specific audience so the room arrives where you did. That is why the format shifts with the audience, and why the seven examples below look so different from each other.

What to include in a sales presentation

The decks below are built from the same short list of slides. Which ones you use depends on who is in the room.

  • Introduction. Your first chance to make an impression. It establishes your credibility and sets the stage for everything after it.
  • Table of contents. Sets the structure of the presentation and gives your audience a roadmap to follow.
  • Objectives and KPIs. What the deck is meant to achieve, in numbers wherever you have them.
  • Product overview. What you sell and how it works, sized for the people in front of you.
  • Stats and figures. Performance and growth metrics on a chart, funnel, or graph, so the raw numbers land quickly.
  • Proof. Company background, past customers, a product demonstration or video, and the results you delivered.
  • Talking points and deliverables. What happens next, and who is waiting on what.

Sales presentation FAQ

What is a sales presentation?

A sales presentation is the slide deck a sales team uses to move a deal or a plan forward. Some are client facing, like a proposal or a case study. Others are internal, like a sales strategy deck or a team stand-up. All of them package your story for one specific audience.

What should a sales presentation include?

Most sales decks pull from the same short list: an introduction, a table of contents, your objectives and KPIs, a product overview, stats and figures on a chart or graph, proof in the form of past customers and results, and the talking points that say what happens next. Which of those you use depends on the audience.

What are the most common types of sales presentations?

The seven on this page cover most of what a sales team builds in a quarter: sales strategy, key account management, sales go to market, case study, sales proposal, sales projection, and team stand-up. Case studies and proposals are the ones a client sees. The rest keep the team aligned on strategy, accounts, and projections before any of it reaches a buyer.

How many slides should a sales presentation have?

There is no fixed number, because the deck is sized for the meeting. A useful rule is to limit each slide to one idea or topic, then order the slides so the presentation flows naturally, usually an overview followed by the specifics.

What makes a good sales presentation?

A straightforward message, a visually impactful design, and specific stats or data that point to your strengths. Charts, graphs, and photos break up the data and keep the room with you, and a template gets the placement and design out of your way so you can spend the time on the story.

Sales presentation FAQ +FAQ schema

What is a sales presentation?

A sales presentation is the slide deck a sales team uses to move a deal or a plan forward. Some are client facing, like a proposal or a case study. Others are internal, like a sales strategy deck or a team stand-up. All of them package your story for one specific audience.

What should a sales presentation include?

Most sales decks pull from the same short list: an introduction, a table of contents, your objectives and KPIs, a product overview, stats and figures on a chart or graph, proof in the form of past customers and results, and the talking points that say what happens next. Which of those you use depends on the audience.

What are the most common types of sales presentations?

The seven on this page cover most of what a sales team builds in a quarter: sales strategy, key account management, sales go to market, case study, sales proposal, sales projection, and team stand-up. Case studies and proposals are the ones a client sees. The rest keep the team aligned on strategy, accounts, and projections before any of it reaches a buyer.

How many slides should a sales presentation have?

There is no fixed number, because the deck is sized for the meeting. A useful rule is to limit each slide to one idea or topic, then order the slides so the presentation flows naturally, usually an overview followed by the specifics.

What makes a good sales presentation?

A straightforward message, a visually impactful design, and specific stats or data that point to your strengths. Charts, graphs, and photos break up the data and keep the room with you, and a template gets the placement and design out of your way so you can spend the time on the story.
