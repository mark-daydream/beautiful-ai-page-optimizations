H2: Pro Tips for a Pictograph Template

Remember these tips when creating a pictograph template slide.

Choose the right icon Pick an that makes sense for the data it will represent. For example, you might use a coin for the number of sales or a person for a number of users.

Keep it simple Icons in your pictograph work best when they're simple and easily understood. If they're too detailed or busy, they'll distract your audience.

Show progress or ratings Pictographs aren't just for displaying statistics. A pictograph example can show the status of a project or the average user rating of a product.

Use shades of the same color Use a darker shade of one color for the percentage you're highlighting, and a lighter shade of the same color for the remaining icons. Contrasting colors look too confusing.

H2: Pictograph template FAQ

Is the pictograph template editable?

Yes. The template is a fully editable Smart Slide. Change your values, icons, and labels, and the layout adapts automatically as you work.

How do I add my own data to the pictograph template?

Open the template and type your values, icons, and labels in, or upload a document or data file and Beautiful.ai places them on the slide.

Can AI fill in the pictograph template for me?

Yes. Describe the pictograph you want to show and the AI assistant fills the template with your values, icons, and labels in place. You can keep editing everything afterward.

Can I export the pictograph template to PowerPoint or Google Slides?

Yes. Slides export to PowerPoint, Google Slides, or PDF, and you can also present or share a live link directly from Beautiful.ai.

When should I use a pictograph?

Use a pictograph when one simple statistic needs to be memorable, such as progress toward a goal, a rating, or a share of a whole. For several series or precise comparisons, a bar graph carries the detail better.

Why: The page now runs the Bunny.net player, so every value here comes from that player. The video ID is the folder in the player’s data-player-src (the playlist.m3u8 URL), contentUrl is Bunny’s MP4 rendition of that ID (play_720p.mp4 is the highest one the encode produced), the first thumbnailUrl is the poster image the player already shows, the second is Bunny’s own frame, duration is read from the HLS playlist and uploadDate is the date Bunny processed the video (swap in the library’s upload date if it differs). Blob-src HLS playback leaves nothing crawlable in the DOM, so this block is what makes the video indexable. One setting to change first. The Bunny zone answers 403 to any request without a Referer header, and Googlebot sends none, so Google cannot fetch contentUrl or the Bunny thumbnail until empty referrers are allowed (or googlebot.com is allowlisted) in the video library’s referrer rules. The Webflow-hosted poster needs no change.
